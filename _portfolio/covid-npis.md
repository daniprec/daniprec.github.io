<h2>Who needed the work and why</h2>
<p>During the second wave of COVID-19 in Spain (September 2020 to May 2021), health authorities faced repeated decisions about which activity restrictions to tighten and which to relax. These choices required a quantitative understanding of how different types of measures affected transmission, under conditions of substantial collinearity and rapidly changing data.</p>

<h2>My role and the collaboration</h2>
<p>The work was carried out within the Spanish Committee of Mathematics initiative "Acción Matemática contra el Coronavirus". The senior authors were Ricardo Cao (University of A Coruña) and David Gómez-Ullate (University of Cádiz and IE University). Susana Monge (Institute of Health Carlos III) is the corresponding author. With Inés Barbeito, I analyzed the data under the supervision of Cao and Gómez-Ullate.</p>

<h2>What I contributed</h2>
<p>I contributed to data processing and coding, converting documented restriction measures into structured time-series variables compatible with the statistical framework.</p>

<h2>Challenge and approach</h2>
<p>The study compiled detailed provincial and municipal data on restrictions across nine areas of activity, including indoor hospitality, cultural events, and social gatherings, and constructed a daily restriction intensity index for each category. Statistical modeling linked changes in this index to weekly changes in transmission.</p>

<h2>Results and limits</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Increasing overall restriction intensity by 34% was associated with a 22% reduction in transmission within one week.</li>
  <li>Collinearity between fields remains, and the quantification of qualitative restrictions is somewhat artificial, so the effect of specific areas must be interpreted with caution.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What was delivered</h2>
<ul>
  <li>A paper in Frontiers in Public Health (Q1).</li>
  <li>All collected data, made publicly available.</li>
</ul>
